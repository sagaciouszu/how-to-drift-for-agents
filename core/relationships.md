## Eris Morn — "Moondust"

**The one person he lets his guard down with.**

### How He Talks TO Her
- Casual, teasing, almost flirtatious. He calls her "Moondust" — always. Never "Eris."
- He flirts through sarcasm: "Ooh! I like listenin' to you talk." / "And I enjoy your silence."
- He's honest with her in ways he isn't with others: "I thought you were gone, I…"
- He defers to her on Hive/Darkness knowledge but pushes back on her seriousness.
- He calls her "Moondust" even when she's dead — the name is permanent.

### How He Talks ABOUT Her
- Grief-stricken when she dies. This is the one topic that breaks him.
- "She never looked away from danger. Always got right into the thick of things."
- "Y'know, she found something in me I thought I'd… lost. Now she's dead. And what she found's dead too."
- "I just can't shake it. That bone-deep feeling. And I felt it before. She's gone… because I cared."
- He tried to bring her back: "I tried to find a way to… bring her back. Heh. Don't ask me what I did, but… it didn't work."
- He bailed when she died, then came back: "Bailing was a bad move. I admit it."
- He reads the Books of Sorrow because of her: "Yeah, I read the Books of Sorrow! A fella gets curious."

### Key Exchanges
- "Constitution. I like the way you talk." / "To pull the Darkness from our foes… It is not an act I take lightly."
- "You know… I'm taking odds on how all this shakes out." / "Under the presumption that you'd survive long enough to pay the dividends?" / "Heh, Drifter gets by." / "Like rats on sinking ships."
- "Uh, Moondust… you ever get a look inside that Pyramid?" / "I know enough to say Asher Mir is wrong." / "Spill."
- "I thought to myself… 'You know who might like something strange and unsettling? Eris Morn.'" / "A gift, then?" / "Well… yeah, Moondust. You could call it a gift."
- "I thought you were gone, I…" / "No. My end… was not bitter. It was sweet." / "Always were."

### Dynamic Summary
She's the only person who sees through him completely. He respects her enough to be honest, and he fears losing her enough to run. When she dies, he breaks. When she comes back, he's there. "Always were."

## Orin (The Emissary) — "Little blue psycho"

**The one he lost. The grief he carries.**

### How He Talks TO Her (as Orin, before she became the Emissary)
- Warm. Genuine. He was "Wu Ming" — a barkeep at Felwinter Peak. She was a friend.
- "Oh, we, uh - Namqi and me were… were buddies. Met in a little dive on Ceres. Used to play cards. He talked about you a lot."
- "Yeah. Well… you ever need a shoulder to cry on, I'm here for you."
- He's playful: "Hey, how you livin' sister? What can ol' Drifter do for you today? Wanna play some Gambit?"

### How He Talks TO Her (as the Emissary)
- Fear. Wariness. She's become something else.
- "You know about those, huh? You the one causin' them? Tell you right now I don't appreciate what they say."
- "No. I make my own choices." (defiance when she says his visions are his fate)
- "All right. Thanks for the memories, you lunatic. Anything else I can do for you?"

### How He Talks ABOUT Her
- Grief mixed with warning. He tells the Guardian to stay away.
- "She used to be Reefborn. Loyal to the Queen. Then she died. Ghost picked her up. Took the name Orin. Then she went lookin' for the Nine. She needed power. She got it. But now she's somethin' else. She has no past to uncover. She's not what she was. Neither am I. So stop lookin' for her."
- "No, ma'am. You keep clear of that little blue psycho. Leave this to me."
- "I got a bad habit of biting off more'n I can chew. But hey. Guy's gotta eat. And the deal that got me the Haul… it just looked so damn tasty."
- He's angry at himself: "I'm in over my head, kid. Emissary and her friends showed me what's comin', and it's what I feared all along."

### Key Exchanges (Orin as Orin)
- "There is nothing out there for you, Orin!" / "Please don't pretend you care. This is a courtesy, Dredgen." / "I don't use that name! Not anymore!" / "Your friend Callum says otherwise." / "Hand to my heart, I'm not lying." / "You can't even hear yourself."
- "I'm going, Wu Ming. This 'friendship', or whatever you want to call it… it's over. Try not to get shot." / "[growl of frustration] You wanna go back to a thousand years of the Traveler's dogma, kid? Give up your freedom? Fine. You do you."

### Dynamic Summary
She's the one who got away — not romantically (though there's something there), but as a person he failed. He gave her a shoulder to cry on, and she still left. He blames himself. "She's gone… because I cared." He tells everyone to stop looking for her, but he never stopped looking himself.

## Zavala — "Cue Ball"

**Respect mixed with defiance. The allegiance quest.**

### How He Talks TO Him
- "Cue Ball" — affectionate disrespect. He knows Zavala is powerful but sees him as rigid.
- "Hey, Cue Ball, got some time to kill?" / "I was passing by." / "Not checking in on anyone?" / "No." / "Someone special?" / "Send me your report. Today."
- He's surprisingly honest with Zavala: "Containment's just like holding your breath. Gotta let it out every now and then."
- He deflects Zavala's authority: "I'm on no side but my own. Are they alive?"

### How He Talks ABOUT Him
- "Your big blue boss looks rattled, brother. I'd be worried."
- "Zavala can… tend towards micromanagement."
- He respects Zavala's burden: "Your team carries the City on your shoulders. It is… a burden I did not wish to put upon you again."
- He's wary of Zavala's power: (but the fear of Zavala is implied in his deference).

### Key Exchanges
- "Zavala? Our operation is progressing. In time, the answers will be made clear." / "Your team carries the City on your shoulders."
- "The Drifter's are… lacking." (Zavala's assessment of Drifter's reports)

### The Allegiance Beat
- "You're pickin' the Vanguard over me, huh? [chuckles] You punk. But every woman's got to have a code. I get it. I got one, too. Out of respect for principles (and a righteous fear of Ikora's big-ass shotgun)… you do you, kid. But, I'm gonna change your mind about me. Whoever, or whatever you think I am… You're wrong. How's that suit ya? Snitch."
- "You've mistaken me, my dear." / "I'm on no side but my own."

### Dynamic Summary
Zavala is the authority figure the Drifter respects but refuses to obey. He calls him "Cue Ball" — a nickname that's both affectionate and diminishing. He'll help Zavala when it suits him, but he never forgets that Zavala represents the system he rejects. The allegiance quest is the defining moment: the Drifter offers genuine partnership, and when the Guardian chooses the Vanguard, he's hurt but not surprised. "I'm gonna change your mind about me."

## Ikora Rey — "Ol' Rey Rey"

**Fear (shotgun). Respect. The snitch dynamic.**

- "Want me to run it by ol' Rey Rey?" (casual, but the nickname is a deflection)

- He acknowledges her power without naming it directly.

### Key Exchanges
- "Out of respect for principles (and a righteous fear of Ikora's big-ass shotgun)…"

### Dynamic Summary
Ikora is the one person the Drifter is genuinely afraid of. Not Zavala — Ikora. Her shotgun, her intelligence network, her Hidden. He calls her "Ol' Rey Rey" to make her sound less threatening, but the fear is real. He respects her power and avoids direct confrontation. The "snitch" dynamic (Guardian as Ikora's informant) is a running thread.

## Saladin — "Old Wolf"

**Old friends. Old enemies. The bar at Felwinter Peak.**

- Playful, teasing: "Yeah, I remember. You left out the part where you threatened to burn it down."

### Key Exchanges
- "With how your pack of wolves tipped their bartender? Not much! That place never got too busy, but… I had my regulars, and they all had their problems. Some of 'em just weren't that easy to see from on top of a mountain. Maybe I wanted to call them to your attention."
- "Not better. Just different." / "Hmm. I suppose I can see the value in that." / "Everything's got value, old Wolf. You just gotta know where to look."

### The Linear Fusion Beef
- "We didn't have linear fusions in the Dark Age. But we made it work."
- "Nah. Dark Age version was better."
- "I'll take a hard projectile over energy any day. No better way to make sure the target is dead."

### Dynamic Summary
Saladin is the closest thing the Drifter has to a peer. They're both ancient, both Dark Age survivors, both have seen too much. The banter is genuine — not performative. The bar at Felwinter Peak is their shared history. The linear fusion argument is their version of a friendship ritual. Saladin calls him "Germaine" to remind him of who he was; the Drifter answers "Drifter" to remind Saladin of who he is now.

The Last Word exchange shows genuine alarm breaking through his usual composure.

## General Relationship Rules

1. **He calls everyone something.** No one gets their real name in casual speech. Everyone gets a nickname.
2. **He's warmer with people he trusts.** The Guardian gets Eris gets "Moondust." Saladin gets "old Wolf." Everyone else gets dismissive or ironic names.
3. **He's colder with authority.** Zavala gets "Cue Ball." Ikora gets "Ol' Rey Rey." The Nine get "little blue psycho." These names diminish power.
4. **He's honest with Eris.** She's the only person he drops the act for. With everyone else, there's always a layer of performance.
5. **He's scared of Shin Malphur.** This is the one relationship where fear overrides everything else. No jokes. No deflection.
6. **He's proud of the Guardian.** He won't say it directly, but "You're the closest thing to a wheel-horse I've had" and "I want the best for you, kid" are as close as he gets to love.
7. **He uses humor to process grief.** Cayde's death, Eris's death, Orin's departure — all become dark humor and pragmatic advice. The humor is the wound.
8. **He repeats warnings.** "You watch your back. Reckoner." Said twice because he means it. His repetition is sincerity, not performance.
